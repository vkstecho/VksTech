🎨 First-Piece Colour Approval Is the Gate That Protects the Whole Run. Skipping It or Approving Casually Creates Expensive Rejections Later.

📖 10-min read · Colour & Quality · Quality + Print Operators + PPC

Colour is one of the first things the customer notices and one of the hardest to recover once the job is running. A disciplined first-piece (or first-roll) colour approval process — against the approved proof or standard — prevents long runs of off-shade material and the arguments that follow.

📌 Core Rules
  • Approve against the signed proof or master standard under agreed lighting
  • Check density / Delta E where instruments are available; visual alone is risky for critical brands
  • Record who approved, time, and any adjustments made
  • If the proof is old or the substrate changed, re-validate before full speed
  • Never “run and adjust later” on brand-critical work

Practical Checklist

  • Proof or standard available and in good condition
  • Lighting conditions correct (or instrument used)
  • First piece / first few metres checked for all colours and register
  • Approval signed or logged before the machine goes to production speed
  • Any deviation noted and either corrected or escalated
💡 Floor Rule: The first-piece colour gate is cheaper than sorting a full roll or facing a customer claim. Make the approval visible and non-negotiable for critical jobs.
