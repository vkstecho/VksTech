❓ Five-Why Stops the Team from Treating Symptoms. Use It on Recurring Converting Defects So the Real Cause Is Fixed Once

📖 11-min read  ·  Problem Solving  ·  Quality + Production + PPC

When the same defect returns week after week — wrinkles, weak bond, registration drift, edge cracks — the usual response is another temporary fix: adjust tension, change viscosity, slow the machine, or blame the material. Five-Why (and similar structured methods) forces the team to keep asking until the real controllable cause is found and fixed. Done well, it turns recurring waste into permanent improvement.

📌 30-Second Snapshot
  • Use Five-Why on recurring or high-cost defects, not on every one-off
  • Ask “why” until you reach a cause the plant can actually control
  • Write the chain down; do not stop at the first convenient answer
  • Verify the fix with data after the change
  • Share the learning so the same defect is not “solved” again next month

1. How to Run a Useful Five-Why

  1. Define the problem clearly — What, where, when, how much. “Bond is low on structure X since week 12” is better than “quality is bad”.
  2. Ask why — Each answer becomes the next question. Keep going until the answer is something the team can change (setting, method, training, design, supplier agreement).
  3. Stop at the controllable cause — “Humidity was high” is only useful if the next step is a control the plant owns. “Supplier material varies” needs a measurable spec and incoming check.
  4. Confirm with data — After the fix, check whether the defect rate actually dropped.
  5. Document and share — One-page summary is enough. Put it where the next shift and the next engineer can find it.

2. Common Pitfalls

Pitfall Better approach
Stopping at the first answerKeep asking until the cause is actionable
Blaming peopleLook for process, method, training or design gaps
No verificationMeasure the defect after the change
No documentationWrite the chain and the fix so learning is not lost

3. When to Use It

  • Same defect appears repeatedly
  • High waste or high claim cost
  • Customer complaint with no clear root cause
  • After a process or material change that created new problems
💡 Floor Rule: If the same defect has been “fixed” three times this quarter, the team has been treating symptoms. Run a proper Five-Why and fix the real cause once.

Five-Why is a thinking discipline more than a form. Use it on the important recurring problems and the plant will stop fighting the same fires.

❓ फाइव-व्हाई टीम को लक्षणों का इलाज करने से रोकता है। आवर्ती कन्वर्टिंग दोषों पर इसका उपयोग करें ताकि असली कारण एक बार ठीक हो

📖 11-मिनट पढ़ें · प्रॉब्लम सॉल्विंग · क्वालिटी + प्रोडक्शन + PPC

जब वही दोष हफ्ते-दर-हफ्ते लौटता है — झुर्रियाँ, कमजोर बॉन्ड, रजिस्ट्रेशन ड्रिफ्ट — सामान्य प्रतिक्रिया एक और अस्थायी फिक्स होती है। फाइव-व्हाई टीम को तब तक पूछने पर मजबूर करता है जब तक असली नियंत्रण योग्य कारण न मिल जाए और ठीक न हो जाए।

📌 मुख्य बातें
  • आवर्ती या उच्च-लागत दोषों पर इस्तेमाल करें
  • “क्यों” तब तक पूछें जब तक प्लांट नियंत्रित कर सके वाला कारण न मिले
  • श्रृंखला लिखें; पहले सुविधाजनक जवाब पर न रुकें
  • बदलाव के बाद डेटा से फिक्स सत्यापित करें
💡 फर्श नियम: अगर वही दोष इस तिमाही में तीन बार “ठीक” हो चुका है तो टीम लक्षणों का इलाज कर रही है। सही फाइव-व्हाई चलाएं और असली कारण एक बार ठीक करें।
